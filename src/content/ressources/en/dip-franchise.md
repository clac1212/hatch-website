## Where the obligation comes from

The DIP was created by French law no. 89-1008 of 31 December 1989, known as the loi Doubin. Its text is now article L330-3 of the Commercial Code, in force in this wording since 21 September 2000.

The article covers anyone who makes a trade name, trademark or sign available while requiring an exclusive or quasi-exclusive commitment. A restaurant franchisor almost always falls into this case. The document must give truthful information, so that the candidate commits with full knowledge.

## The 20-day period

The DIP and the draft contract are provided at least 20 days before signing. If you ask for a sum before signing, for example to reserve a territory, the period runs before that payment. In that case, the law also requires the services provided in exchange, and each party's obligations if one withdraws, to be set out in writing.

In practice, start from the target signing date and work back:

1. Set the signing date with the candidate.
2. Deliver the DIP and the draft contract at least 20 days earlier, and keep dated proof of delivery.
3. [Take no reservation deposit](/en/resources/redevance-franchise-restauration) before the period is over.
4. If something important changes before signing, deliver a corrected DIP and ask your lawyer whether the period must start again.

To date the delivery, you can have the DIP signed electronically, with a time stamp. Cerca launched this service with Yousign in 2018.

## What the DIP must contain

Article R330-1, last amended by decree no. 2023-1394 of 30 December 2023, in the version in force since 1 January 2024, sets the minimum list.

| Section               | What the text requires                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Watch point for restaurants                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1° Identity           | Registered office, business, legal form, directors, share capital where relevant                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | The information concerns the company offering the contract                                                          |
| 2° Trademark          | Registration or filing date and number, and licence term if the mark is licensed                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Check that the mark covers restaurant services                                                                      |
| 3° Banks              | Bank details, which may be limited to the five main banks                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Nothing specific to the sector                                                                                      |
| 4° History and market | Creation date, key milestones, directors' experience (5 years may suffice), general and local state of the market, annual accounts for the last two financial years                                                                                                                                           | The local market of the target area, as well as the national trend                                                  |
| 5° Network            | List of members and their agreed operating model, addresses of operators under similar contracts (the 50 closest to the planned site if the network has more than 50), number of operators that left in the year before delivery, outlets in the area selling the same products with the franchisor's consent | The number of restaurants that left last year, stating whether the contract expired, was terminated or was annulled |
| 6° Contract           | Term, renewal, termination, assignment, scope of exclusivity                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | The exclusive territory, including takeaway and delivery if you cover them                                          |
| Pre-opening spending  | Nature and amount of brand-specific spending and investment required [before opening](/en/resources/ouvrir-restaurant-franchise)                                                                                                                                                                              | Fit-out, required kitchen equipment, signage: everything specific to the concept                                    |

For a restaurant network, the last row matters most. Candidates compare brands on what they must spend before the first service. A vague or outdated figure weakens the whole document.

## The most common mistakes

### Taking money too early

If you ask for a sum, the DIP must have been delivered 20 days before that payment. A reservation cheque cashed ten days after delivery is out of time, even if signing is planned much later.

### An outdated DIP

The accounts must be those of the last two financial years. The number of departures covers the year before delivery. A DIP reused from one year to the next becomes wrong without anyone noticing.

### The same restaurant list for everyone

Above 50 operators, the text refers to the 50 businesses closest to the planned location. The list therefore changes with the candidate's city.

### Forgetting the draft contract

The 20-day period applies to the DIP and the draft contract together.

### Relying on the acknowledgement of receipt

A statement signed by the candidate does not prove the DIP was complete. In a ruling of 10 January 2018 (no. 15-25.287), the Cour de cassation overturned an appeal court that had relied on the franchisee's statement and experience without checking for itself that the DIP complied with article L330-3.

### Mixing up the DIP and a forecast

Neither L330-3 nor R330-1 requires a financial forecast. The DIP contains a state of the market, not a market study or a revenue promise. The safest route is to let the candidate build their forecast with their accountant, from the network figures you share. If you provide forecast figures yourself, have your lawyer review them: in a dispute, the court examines them to decide whether the franchisee was mistaken about profitability.

## What the franchisor risks

Under criminal law, article R330-2 punishes late or missing delivery with a 5th-class petty offence fine. The Criminal Code caps it at €1,500, and €3,000 for a repeat offence where the text provides for it, which is the case here. For a company, the cap is five times higher, €7,500 (Criminal Code, art. 131-41).

Under civil law, a franchisee may argue that missing or inaccurate information misled them or amounts to fraud (dol). The Civil Code (art. 1130) recognises these defects of consent when, without them, the party would not have contracted or would have contracted on substantially different terms. Annulment claims rest on these defects. The Cour de cassation holds that a breach of article L330-3 leads to annulment only if it vitiated consent (Cass. com., 7 July 2004, no. 02-15.950). The franchisee must therefore prove that the missing or inaccurate information weighed on their decision.

## Before you send your next DIP

Check the number of departures, the accounts and the pre-opening investment amount. Check the list of the 50 closest restaurants for the candidate's city. Record the delivery date, then count 20 days before any signing or payment.
