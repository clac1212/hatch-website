## What the rules say

The PMS is your own system, with no official form to fill in. It gathers EU obligations: good hygiene practices, procedures based on HACCP principles, traceability and withdrawal or recall procedures. The French Ministry of Agriculture defines it in three parts: good hygiene practices, HACCP procedures, then traceability and non-conformity management.

The rules take the size of the business into account. The EU regulation asks for documents and records "according to the nature and size of the business". The ministry allows lighter requirements where five people or fewer work in contact with food. A restaurant must still carry out a hazard analysis, even a simplified one, and it can base it entirely on a validated good hygiene practice guide. The guide must match your activity exactly, be available on the premises and be applied. For restaurants, that is the "Restaurateur" guide (November 2015 version); fast food also has its own guide, validated in 2024.

## 1. Good hygiene practices

This is the foundation, without which nothing else holds. For a small restaurant, they cover:

- staff hygiene: clothing, handwashing, health;
- the cleaning and disinfection plan: what, with which product, how often, by whom;
- pest control;
- the cold and hot chain, with temperature records;
- [receiving goods](/en/resources/controle-factures-fournisseurs-restaurant);
- managing stock, waste and allergens.

In a small restaurant, not every instruction has to be written down. The ministry accepts oral procedures, provided everyone concerned can explain them to the inspector: failing to do so is a non-conformity. Deviations and corrective actions, however, are always recorded. For temperatures, the ministry's sheet for restaurants expects at least daily monitoring of each unit, and continuous recording for freezers holding frozen food.

Storage temperatures are set by the French order of 21 December 2009. Some useful values for restaurants:

| Food                                                      | Temperature on the premises |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Minced meat                                               | +2°C max                    |
| Fresh fishery products                                    | 0 to +2°C (melting ice)     |
| Dishes prepared in advance                                | +3°C max                    |
| Poultry, meat preparations                                | +4°C max                    |
| Ice cream, frozen minced meat and frozen fishery products | -18°C max                   |
| Other frozen food                                         | -12°C max                   |
| Dishes served hot (hot holding)                           | +63°C min                   |

Other temperatures are allowed if they rest on a validated good practice guide or a validated hazard analysis. The often-quoted rule of cooling from +63°C to +10°C in under two hours sits in the order's annex for institutional catering: in a commercial restaurant, follow what your guide or your hazard analysis sets.

## 2. The HACCP plan

HACCP is a seven-principle method: analyse hazards, identify critical points, set limits, monitor, correct, verify, document. In a small restaurant, the analysis may conclude that good practices are enough for most steps. Where a critical point exists, such as cooking or cooling a dish, its monitoring must be recorded. A long list of critical points is often a sign that basic good practices are not in place: the HACCP plan does not replace them.

## 3. Traceability

You must be able to say where each product came from and when it arrived. In practice: keep delivery notes and invoices, keep the labels of products you unpack, and note batches when you freeze or prepare ahead.

How long should these documents be kept? No text sets a general retention period for traceability. The ministry's technical instruction on the PMS gives these benchmarks:

| Type of document                                                                                                  | Retention                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Traceability, recommended general rule                                                                            | 5 years from production or delivery   |
| Traceability of a perishable product (use-by date under 3 months, or no date: fruit, vegetables, unpackaged food) | 6 months after delivery or production |
| Own-check results (lab tests)                                                                                     | 3 years                               |

For temperature logs and non-conformity sheets, these texts set no period: the simplest is to keep them as long as your traceability documents.

## 4. Handling non-conformities

A non-conformity is a deviation: a fridge at the wrong temperature, a product delivered damaged, a skipped cleaning task. The ministry distinguishes two responses. A correction removes the deviation (you throw the product away). A corrective action removes its cause (you get the fridge repaired, you change the rota). Record the deviation and what you did on the same document, with the date and the person. No format is required: a sheet, a notebook or a diary will do. Also plan what to do if a supplier recalls a product.

## A small restaurant's PMS in practice

An example set-up, to adapt to your kitchen and your good practice guide.

| Part             | Document                                         | Who                         | Frequency (example)                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Good practices   | Cleaning and disinfection plan                   | Each station                | Daily, and weekly for deep cleaning |
| Good practices   | Cold storage temperature log                     | Opening and closing         | Twice a day                         |
| Good practices   | Receiving check (temperatures, dates, condition) | Whoever receives the goods  | Every delivery                      |
| HACCP            | Cooking and cooling log                          | The cook                    | Every relevant batch                |
| Traceability     | File of delivery notes and labels                | The manager                 | Every delivery                      |
| Non-conformities | Non-conformity sheet                             | Whoever spots the deviation | As soon as it happens               |
| Allergens        | Written information available to guests          | The manager                 | Every menu change                   |

## What the inspector checks

An inspection covers two things. First, the rules themselves: the state of the premises, equipment and practices. Then your PMS: the inspector checks that you actually apply it, through your documents, your records and what the team can explain. If the team does not know good practices, the inspection leads to a training requirement.

The inspector will also ask for:

- proof of hygiene training, which must be kept on the premises;
- allergen information: in writing, on the menu, a board or a notebook guests can access.

The result is published on Alim'confiance on four levels: very satisfactory, satisfactory (a reminder letter may follow), needs improvement (corrective measures within a set deadline, then a new inspection), needs urgent correction (the authority orders administrative closure, or withdrawal or suspension of the approval). A restaurant may display its level in its window.

## The mandatory hygiene training

Commercial restaurants in France must have at least one person on staff trained in food hygiene (article L233-4 of the Rural Code). This covers traditional restaurants, cafeterias and self-service outlets, and fast food.

- Length: 14 hours, including at least two hours in person per seven-hour block, for hands-on practice with equipment (order of 12 February 2024).
- Provider: authorised by the regional prefect. The list is published twice a year on the regional agriculture directorate's website.
- Exemptions: three years' experience as a manager or operator of a food business, or a qualification listed in the order of 18 November 2024 and awarded after 1 January 2006.
- Validity: no expiry date and no renewal requirement.
- Proof: the certificate or qualification must be kept on the premises; experience must be provable at inspection.

This training does not replace the rest. The person running the PMS must be trained in applying it, and everyone who handles food must receive instructions or training suited to their job. The rules require no periodic refresher.

## In a restaurant network

Each franchisee is the operator of their restaurant: they answer for its PMS to the inspector. Head office can provide a common base (procedures, record sheets, target temperatures for its recipes), but each restaurant adapts it to its kitchen. Before an opening, check that the training certificate is on site and that the record sheets are in use from the first service. Then build the PMS points into your visits and audits: a gap shows up earlier than at the official inspection.
