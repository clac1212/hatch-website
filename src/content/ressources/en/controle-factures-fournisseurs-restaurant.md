## The three documents to match

| Document                                     | What it proves                          | What you check                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purchase order (or message, or supplier app) | What you asked for, at the agreed price | Products, quantities, unit prices, expected delivery date       |
| Delivery note                                | What arrived, and your reservations     | Actual quantities, substituted products, written reservations   |
| Invoice                                      | What you are asked to pay               | Delivery note quantities, agreed prices, discounts, VAT, totals |

If you order in writing with a number, keep it: in France it must appear on the invoice when the buyer issued one.

## On delivery: the checklist

Do it in front of the driver, before signing. Five minutes are enough once it is a routine.

- Count and weigh. Number of boxes, weight of meat and fish, number of items. Compare with the delivery note, not from memory.
- Check the products. Same reference as the order, same size, same origin if you show it on the menu. A substituted product must be flagged.
- Check the cold chain. Measure the temperature of fresh and frozen products with a thermometer. In France, the order of 21 December 2009 sets the reference points: +2 °C at most for fresh fish and minced meat, +4 °C for poultry and meat preparations, +3 °C for ready-made dishes, −18 °C for ice cream and for frozen minced meat or fish. [Your food safety plan](/en/resources/plan-maitrise-sanitaire-restaurant) sets the limit for each product.
- Read the dates. A use-by date too close for your turnover: flag it on the note, and refuse the product if you will not be able to use it in time.
- Look at the packaging. Crushed box, punctured vacuum pack, thawed product: refuse it or write a reservation.
- Keep the labels of products you unpack. They are part of traceability.
- Put fresh and frozen goods away first, then do the rest.

## Writing a reservation on the delivery note

A vague reservation proves nothing. "Subject to unpacking" describes no discrepancy. Write what is missing or wrong, with figures, then sign and have the driver countersign if possible. Take a photo of the note and of the product.

Useful reservations:

- "Minced beef: 10 kg delivered instead of 12 kg ordered."
- "2 boxes of cream out of 6 crushed, refused."
- "Fresh salmon delivered at 7 °C on the thermometer (limit: +2 °C), refused."

When goods arrive through a carrier, the French Commercial Code (article L133-3) sets a strict rule: taking delivery ends any claim against the carrier for damage or partial loss unless the recipient notifies the carrier of a reasoned protest, by registered letter or by a commissaire de justice (bailiff), within three days (public holidays excluded). This rule covers the carrier. When the supplier delivers with its own truck, its general terms of sale set the claim period: read them once and note that period. Either way, also tell the supplier in writing the same day.

## Matching the invoice

When the invoice arrives, put it next to the delivery note and the order. Check, line by line:

1. invoiced quantities against delivered quantities, reservations included;
2. unit prices against the agreed price or the latest price list;
3. promised discounts;
4. refused products, which must not appear;
5. duplicates, when one delivery is invoiced twice.

Example, with made-up figures:

| Line                       | Ordered        | Delivered (note)            | Invoiced       | Gap excl. VAT        |
| -------------------------- | -------------- | --------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------- |
| Minced beef                | 12 kg at €9.80 | 10 kg (written reservation) | 12 kg at €9.80 | €19.60               |
| Cream                      | 6 L at €4.20   | 6 L                         | 6 L at €4.60   | €2.40                |
| Butter                     | 5 kg at €8.50  | 5 kg                        | 5 kg at €8.50  | €0                   |
| **Credit note to request** |                |                             |                | **€22.00 excl. VAT** |

Twenty-two euros looks small. With one delivery a week, [the same gap repeated over a year comes to more than €1,100 excluding VAT](/en/resources/ratio-matiere-restaurant).

## Getting the credit note

Send the request to the supplier in writing as soon as you see the gap, with:

- the invoice number and date;
- the delivery note number, with the reservation and the photo;
- the itemised amount of each discrepancy, as in the table above;
- the amount of the credit note requested.

In France, an issued invoice can no longer be changed directly. While it is unpaid, the supplier can issue a corrective invoice with a new number and the wording "annule et remplace la facture n°…" (cancels and replaces invoice no.). Once the invoice is paid, any correction goes through a credit note. The credit note has its own number and follows the same numbering rules as invoices, either in the invoice series or in a series kept for credit notes.

File the credit note with the invoice it corrects and pass both on to your accountant. Invoices issued or received must be kept for ten years.

## What e-invoicing changes

Since 1 September 2026, every business in France must be able to receive electronic invoices, through an approved platform of its choice. Large and mid-sized companies already issue all their invoices electronically. Small and micro businesses have until 1 September 2027 to do the same. The obligation covers invoices between businesses: your sales to private customers are not affected, but their data will have to be reported to the tax authority (e-reporting) on the same timetable.

For you, this means invoices from suppliers that are large or mid-sized companies already arrive through your platform, with new mandatory details: your SIREN number, the delivery address if it differs from your address, and the nature of the transaction (supply of goods or services). The checks stay the same. The delivery address, though, becomes one more thing to check if you have several sites.
