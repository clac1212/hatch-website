## The three sums, and what each pays for

The entry fee is a one-off sum paid at signing. It usually covers the transfer of the concept and know-how, initial training and opening support. It can be paid at once or in instalments, for example a deposit to reserve a territory before signing, then the balance at signing.

The operating royalty is paid every month. According to the Observatoire, it pays for the franchisor's support: network management, training, technical and commercial assistance. It is either a percentage of turnover or a fixed amount.

The advertising fee funds the network's communication. Not every network charges one, and some calculate it on turnover like the operating royalty. It does not automatically cover the restaurant's local marketing: the contract should say who pays for it. Sooner or later franchisees will ask what the money collected was spent on: present the budget and its use every year.

## What the law says

No article of the French Commercial Code caps these amounts. The text acts on disclosure and timing:

- [The DIP and the draft contract must be delivered at least 20 days](/en/resources/dip-franchise) before signing, or before any payment required before signing (art. L330-3). If a deposit is required before signing, for example to reserve a territory, the 20 days run before that payment.
- If a sum is paid before signing, the services provided in exchange and each party's obligations if one withdraws are set out in writing (art. L330-3).
- The DIP states the nature and amount of brand-specific spending and investment the franchisee must commit before opening (art. R330-1).
- The DIP includes the franchisor's annual accounts for the last two financial years (art. R330-1).
- The Commercial Code penalises obtaining from a partner an advantage with no consideration or one clearly out of proportion, or subjecting them to obligations that create a significant imbalance (art. L442-1). A royalty that matches no real service can be challenged on that basis.

## Why there is no benchmark rate

There is no official average for entry fees or royalties, neither for restaurants nor for franchising as a whole. The French Franchise Federation publishes the number of brands and outlets every year, not their financial terms. The ranges on specialist sites vary widely from one source to another and do not state their method: they are not enough to set a rate.

To place your brand, compare the DIPs and draft contracts of a few direct competitors instead, on the same turnover. And start from your own costs: what recruiting, training, opening and supporting a restaurant costs you. A rate copied from a competitor does not tell you whether your services are paid at their price. For market size, the French Franchise Federation counts 298 quick-service restaurant brands and 109 themed restaurant brands in 2025, with 9,430 and 1,840 franchised outlets.

## How a royalty is calculated

The operating royalty is most often a percentage of the franchisee's turnover, sometimes a fixed amount. The calculation is simple: on €100,000 of turnover excluding VAT, a 5% royalty comes to €5,000. The rules around that rate vary from one contract to another.

Before you set or review your fees, write down:

1. The base: turnover excluding or including VAT, with or without delivery and takeaway, with or without sales made through platforms.
2. The minimum and the cap: a fixed monthly floor or a cap, if any, and when they apply after opening. If you plan a reduced rate at the start, write down how long it lasts.
3. The frequency: monthly or quarterly, based on which turnover statement, and how it is paid (direct debit, transfer).
4. What each fee covers: ongoing training, visits, tools, national or local communication.
5. Other costs: required software, training for new staff, purchases from approved suppliers.

## What a candidate compares, and what you must be able to show

A candidate weighs what they pay against what they get. Three questions come up:

### How much must be spent [before the first service](/en/resources/ouvrir-restaurant-franchise)?

Entry fee, brand-specific investment disclosed in the DIP, opening communication budget. Show the full entry ticket, initial training included, because that is the sum candidates compare.

### What is paid every month?

Operating royalty, advertising fee, fixed costs. Add them up on the same turnover, or the comparison means nothing.

### What does the head office do in return?

This is the hardest question. The royalty pays for support. If your network cannot show its visits, training and communications, the rate will always look too high.

Also bear in mind that entry fees do not keep a network alive. They mostly cover recruitment and opening; royalties, and where relevant the margin on products, fund support over time. A franchisor that relies on entry fees has to keep recruiting, at the risk of lowering its standards.

## Before you set your fees

Have the structure reviewed by your accountant and your lawyer. Check that every sum appears in the DIP and the draft contract, and that none is collected less than 20 days after they are delivered.
