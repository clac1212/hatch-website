## What the manual must cover

The manual is the written record of your know-how. The EU regulation on vertical agreements (EU 2022/720, art. 1(1)(j)) defines know-how as a "secret, substantial and identified" body of practical information. "Identified" means described comprehensively enough to check the other two criteria. The French Franchise Federation (FFF) puts it this way: know-how is written down in one or more operations manuals. Because it is secret, hand it over after the contract is signed, under a confidentiality clause, and limit access to each part according to each person's role.

In food service, the manual also meets each restaurant's hygiene obligations. The food safety plan, HACCP principles, food traceability and information on the 14 regulated allergens are the operator's responsibility. The network manual does not replace them. It says how to apply them with your products and equipment.

## Two separate documents

Putting everything into one 200-page file is the most common mistake. The franchisee and the crew member do not have the same questions.

|                 | Franchisee manual                                              | Station cards                                         |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Reader          | The franchisee, the restaurant manager                         | Crew, in the kitchen or front of house                |
| Content         | Concept, brand, management, hiring, relations with head office | One task, one recipe, one check, one cleaning routine |
| Length          | Several chapters                                               | One page, one phone screen                            |
| When it is read | Before opening, then when in doubt                             | During service, at the station                        |
| Updates         | A few times a year                                             | With every recipe or equipment change                 |

The franchisee manual can stay long. A card must fit on a screen.

## The format of a card staff actually read

A card answers one question. An example at BunBun, the fictional burger brand Hatch OS uses for demos, with the card "Check the fryer oil":

- The title is the question: "When do I change the fryer oil?".
- Numbered steps, in the imperative: "Dip the strip", "Compare the colour", "Log the result".
- Exact values, never "enough" or "regularly". Here, the French legal limit: above 25% polar compounds, oil is unfit for consumption.
- One photo per key step, taken in a restaurant of the network.
- The why in one line, because a rule people understand is better applied.
- A footer: version number, date, owner at head office.

Use the same word for the same object across cards. If one card says "holding tray" and another "hot tray", staff look for two objects.

## [Language: write plainly, then translate](/en/resources/former-equipiers-reseau-restauration)

French labour law provides that any document needed to carry out the job is written in French. The same article allows translations into one or more foreign languages. The rule applies to the employer, so to the franchisee for its own staff. For your cards, that means one French master version with translations derived from it.

Writing for translation helps native speakers too. One sentence, one idea. No in-house abbreviations. Numbers rather than adjectives. A short card translates without losing its meaning.

## One version that counts

A manual loses its value as soon as two versions circulate. Three rules are enough:

1. Each card has an owner at head office and a visible update date.
2. A new version replaces the old one everywhere. The old one is archived and removed from the folder.
3. A card is reviewed at every trigger: new recipe, new equipment, [a gap found in an audit](/en/resources/grille-audit-hygiene-restaurant), a question that keeps coming back from the field.

Flag what changed from one version to the next, and have a franchisee review major changes before they go out: they will see what will not work during service.

## Put the manual where staff work

The binder in the office helps on opening day, not during the rush. During service, staff have their hands full and do not always carry their phone. Two formats complement each other: [a shared tablet in the kitchen](/en/agents/owl), and each person's phone to reread a card before a shift.

Whatever the format, write down what teams ask. A question that comes back every week points to a missing or badly written card.

## Which tools to share the manual

Four families of tools exist, and they do not solve the same problem.

- Document tools (Google Drive, Notion, SharePoint): easy for head office to maintain, impractical to consult in the middle of service.
- Franchise management platforms: they have their own knowledge base. Cerca, for example, offers natural-language search in its base, Cerca Answers. Documents are then brought into the platform.
- General-purpose AI assistants: you can load documents into a Project in Claude or a Gem in Gemini (replaced by "skills" on personal Google accounts from November 2026), and the assistant answers from those files. A Gem linked to Google Drive follows the latest version of the files. It is cheap and powerful. The limits for the field: every user needs an account, and sharing a Claude Project with the whole team requires a Team or Enterprise plan. ChatGPT has been reachable on WhatsApp again in the European Union since July 2026, but as an individual chat: the head office cannot send or track anything through it.
- Specialised agents for networks, which answer the field on its own channels from existing documents.

## Where to start

1. List the twenty questions your managers get most often.
2. Write a one-page card for each, in the format above.
3. Have them reviewed by a newly hired team member. Whatever they do not understand needs rewriting.
4. Name an owner for each card and date every version.
5. Put the cards on the tablet and on phones, then track questions for a month.

The manual does not need to be complete on day one. It needs to be accurate, dated and easy to find.
