## Start from what inspectors check

In France, the results of official food safety inspections are public on Alim'confiance. Each inspected establishment gets one of four hygiene levels: very satisfactory, satisfactory, needs improvement, urgent correction needed. The last level covers non-compliance that may endanger consumer health, leading to administrative closure, withdrawal or suspension of the health approval. The result stays visible for one year after the inspection.

Your internal checklist should first spot what would push a restaurant into the bottom two levels. Brand standards come next.

## Families of control points

A readable checklist groups its points by family. Here is a starting point for quick-service restaurants, with the French reference text where one exists.

| Family             | Examples of checkable points                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Reference                                                              |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Staff training     | At least one person in the establishment has food hygiene training (14 h), a diploma from the official list or three years' experience as manager or operator of a food business; proof kept on site                                                                              | Art. L233-4, French Rural Code; Regulation (EC) 852/2004               |
| Staff hygiene      | Clean uniform, equipped handwash station, no jewellery in the kitchen                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Regulation (EC) 852/2004, Annex II                                     |
| Cold and hot chain | Fridge and freezer temperatures checked and logged; maximums respected (+2 °C for minced meat and fresh fish, +4 °C for meat preparations and highly perishable food); hot dishes at +63 °C minimum; rapid cooling of preparations, using the method set in your food safety plan | French order of 21 December 2009, Annex I                              |
| Traceability       | Labels and delivery notes kept until the product is used up; preparations labelled                                                                                                                                                                                                | Regulation (EC) 178/2002, art. 18; order of 21 December 2009           |
| Fryer oil          | Test done and logged; oil changed above 25% polar compounds                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | French decree 2008-184, art. 8                                         |
| Allergens          | Written information on the 14 allergens, displayed or available on request, matching the current menu                                                                                                                                                                             | Regulation (EU) 1169/2011, art. 44; art. R412-14, French Consumer Code |
| Food safety plan   | Plan available, HACCP procedures followed, microbiological tests at the frequency set by the operator                                                                                                                                                                             | Regulation (EC) 852/2004, art. 5                                       |
| Brand standards    | Recipe matches the card, dining room cleanliness, welcome                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Your operations manual                                                 |

Keep the checklist short. A point no auditor can check in under a minute should be rewritten or removed.

## Weight by severity

Not all gaps are equal. A damaged cold-room door seal does not weigh the same as a poster in the wrong place. Put each point into one of three levels:

- Critical: direct risk to customer health (broken cold chain, oil over the limit, undeclared allergen).
- Major: a gap that makes a risk likely if it lasts (missing temperature logs, unlabelled preparation).
- Minor: a standards gap with no health risk (display, presentation).

An example scale: 10 points per critical point, 5 per major point, 1 per minor point. Add a blocking rule: a critical gap triggers same-day action and a follow-up visit, even if the overall score stays good.

## Calculate the score out of 100

The score is the share of points earned out of applicable points, scaled to 100. A point that does not apply (no fryer, no delivery that day) leaves the calculation instead of counting as passed.

A fictional example at BunBun, the Hatch OS demo brand. The checklist has 4 critical points (40 points), 8 major (40 points) and 20 minor (20 points), so 100 applicable points. The restaurant misses one major and three minor points: it scores 92 out of 100. If it also misses one critical point, it drops to 82 and the blocking rule triggers a follow-up visit. Without weighting, the critical gap would have counted as much as a poster in the wrong place.

## Write checkable points

Two auditors should score the same restaurant the same way. That depends mostly on wording.

| Badly written        | Checkable                                                                                          |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Clean kitchen        | Worktop and floor of the cooking area free of residue at the time of the audit                     |
| Good cold management | Temperature of each unit logged at the frequency set in your food safety plan, for the last 7 days |
| Allergens displayed  | Information on the 14 allergens available for every item on the current menu                       |
| Oil OK               | Today's oil test logged, result below 25% polar compounds                                          |

Ask for a photo of every gap. It avoids arguments when the report comes out and becomes the starting point for proof of correction.

## Turn every gap into an action

An audit that stops at the report lets the same gap come back at the next visit. Every gap should leave the audit with:

1. a precise action, written as a task;
2. an owner appointed by the franchisee;
3. a deadline tied to severity, for example same day for critical, within the week for major, by the next visit for minor;
4. an expected proof (photo, document, log);
5. validation by the auditor, which alone closes the action.

Address the action plan to the franchisee: they remain the business owner and the employer of their team, and they are the one who assigns the tasks. End the visit with what works and one or two priorities, without a list of complaints.

## Set the frequency

Frequency is decided restaurant by restaurant, based on risk. Three simple rules:

- a base rhythm that is the same across the network and known to franchisees;
- a follow-up visit soon after any critical gap;
- a self-assessment by the restaurant between visits, on a short version of the checklist.

Official inspections are, as a rule, carried out without prior notice (Regulation (EU) 2017/625, art. 9). Some of your visits can be unannounced too, to see the restaurant as it really runs. Write the right to audit, its rhythm and unannounced visits into the contract or the manual, so franchisees do not discover them on the day.

## Update the checklist

Review the checklist at least once a year, and with every change of menu, equipment or regulation. Look above all at points that fail in several restaurants: a gap repeated everywhere points to [a manual card to revise](/en/resources/manuel-operatoire-franchise-restauration) or [a training to redo](/en/resources/former-equipiers-reseau-restauration), not to a careless franchisee.
