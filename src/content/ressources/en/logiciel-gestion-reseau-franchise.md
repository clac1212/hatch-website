## The question to ask before comparing

A restaurant network does not face the same constraints as a retail network. In the kitchen, staff have their hands full, switch stations during the day and do not always read the head office's language. The head office needs to know that information arrived, was read and is being applied.

Before any demo, write two lists. On one side, what the head office has to manage: franchisee recruitment, contracts, royalties, audits. On the other, what the field needs every day: a procedure, a recipe, an announcement, a training module. The first list points to a management platform. The second raises a simpler question: where will your teams look for the answer?

## 1. All-in-one franchise management platforms

This is the best-known family. One platform, built for the head office, covers the whole franchisee lifecycle.

In France, Cerca (formerly Franchise on Cloud) offers eight modules, from network engagement to automated royalties, in an all-inclusive subscription priced per location, with a 30-day free trial and a mobile app. Seenaps, part of the 6TM group, brings recruitment, network engagement and performance management onto one platform, with AI, e-commerce and ERP integration services. Internationally, FranConnect has a dedicated, English-language offer for multi-location restaurant brands that includes food safety.

These platforms keep everything in one place, royalty billing included. They are opening up to WhatsApp too: in June 2026 Cerca added a WhatsApp Business integration to talk with franchise candidates. The limit comes from daily use. Field teams have to open one more app or intranet. None of them publishes prices.

## 2. The tools you already have

Many networks run on Google Drive or Notion for the operations manual, WhatsApp groups for announcements and a spreadsheet for audits. It costs little and everyone knows how to use it.

The limits show as the network grows. Nobody knows which version of the manual is current, an important announcement gets lost between two delivery photos, and the head office cannot tell who read what.

## 3. General-purpose AI assistants: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

This is the newest family, and often the first one franchisors try. You load the operations manual into a custom GPT in ChatGPT, a Project in Claude or a Gem in Gemini (replaced by "skills" on personal Google accounts from November 2026), and the assistant answers questions from those documents. OpenAI's business plans do not use company data to train their models by default.

It is powerful and cheap for the head office. For the field, three limits keep coming up:

- Everyone has their own access. To query the manual loaded into a GPT, a Project or a Gem, you need an account and the vendor's app. ChatGPT has been reachable on WhatsApp again in the European Union since July 2026, after a European Commission decision against Meta's policy, but each team member chats with the assistant alone: the head office cannot send anything through it.
- They are personal assistants, not network tools. They do not send targeted announcements, track who read them, or run audits.
- In everyday use, the head office cannot see the questions asked, so it does not know where the manual has gaps. And documents are usually copied into the assistant, so every manual update means uploading them again. A Gem linked to Google Drive files is the exception: it follows their latest version.

For a founder looking for an answer for themselves, these tools are often enough. To get fifty restaurants working from the same information, the network layer is missing.

## 4. Specialised AI agents for networks: Hatch OS

Hatch OS is built for the field first. [Your documents stay where they are](/en/agents/jay) (Google Drive, Notion, Airtable, OneDrive or SharePoint, PDF), and each network function has its own agent:

- [Peep answers restaurants on WhatsApp](/en/agents/peep) from your documents, in each team member's language. Anything that is not in your documents goes to the head office instead of being made up. It also sends announcements, targeted by region or format, and tracks reading restaurant by restaurant.
- [Owl answers on the restaurant tablet](/en/agents/owl), by voice or text, during service.
- Jay connects your documents and helps keep them up to date.
- [Pecker trains staff](/en/agents/pecker) through a link sent on WhatsApp, with no app or password, and follows up with those who have not finished.
- [Finch runs audits](/en/agents/finch) on mobile, calculates a score out of 100 and tracks every corrective action.

Hatch OS does not handle royalties, accounting or point of sale. Pricing depends on the agents chosen and the size of the network, and is presented during the demo. ## How to choose

If your head office spends most of its time on contracts, royalties and recruitment, an all-in-one platform makes sense. If you have a handful of restaurants and know every team, your current tools and an AI assistant may be enough. If your problem is that the field never opens the tools, test first what your staff see: where do they ask their question, and in which language do they get the answer?

Either way, ask in the demo who answers on a Saturday night during the rush, and what has to be redone to open a new restaurant. Also check that the tool connects to the ones you keep (documents, point of sale, recruitment): an isolated tool ends up bypassed by the field.
