## What turnover changes for training

According to the AKTO observatory, 60% of employees in French quick-service restaurants are 29 or younger, and the all-round crew member is the most requested job in the sector's job offers, where staff turnover remains high. In hotels, cafés and restaurants, one employee in two is under 35. With teams this young and mobile, training organised once a quarter arrives too late for some crew.

Three practical consequences:

- the onboarding path starts on day one, triggered by the crew member's arrival;
- the first modules cover what protects the customer and the crew member: hygiene, allergens, safety at the station;
- the rest comes station by station, when the crew member is assigned to it.

## What the law already requires

Several French and EU obligations frame training in restaurants. They set a minimum without saying how to train.

| Obligation                                                             | Who it applies to                             | Text                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hygiene instructions or training suited to the job                     | All staff handling food                       | Regulation (EC) 852/2004                                                                           |
| Specific hygiene training (14 h), a diploma or three years' experience | At least one person per commercial restaurant | Art. L233-4, French Rural and Maritime Fishing Code; duration set by the order of 12 February 2024 |
| Adapting employees to their job                                        | The employer, so the franchisee for its teams | Art. L6321-1, French Labour Code                                                                   |
| Documents needed for the job written in French, translations allowed   | The employer                                  | Art. L1321-6, French Labour Code                                                                   |

So the 14-hour course is not enough to train the whole team: it covers one person. Other crew members need instructions suited to their station.

In a franchise, the franchisee employs the crew, so training them is the franchisee's job. Head office's role is to supply ready-to-use content and, during the franchisee's own initial training, to prepare them to pass it on.

## Split training into five-minute modules

Crew members train between services or during their break. A long module ends up abandoned. A good module covers a single task, with one or two photos or a short video, and ends with three questions.

An example onboarding path at BunBun, the fictional burger brand Hatch OS uses for demos:

| When   | Module                                     | Check                                     |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Day 1  | Welcome, uniform, handwashing              | 3-question quiz                           |
| Day 2  | The 14 allergens and the BunBun menu       | 3-question quiz                           |
| Day 3  | Fryer station: cooking and oil check       | Quiz, then a demonstration to the manager |
| Day 5  | Till station: payment and complaints       | Quiz                                      |
| Week 3 | Station assessment with the network's grid | Grid signed by the manager                |

[Modules reuse the cards from the operations manual.](/en/resources/manuel-operatoire-franchise-restauration) A changed card means the matching module gets updated.

## Train on the crew member's phone

In restaurants, crew are almost never in front of a computer. The phone is the only screen they carry. Every step added before the content (installing an app, creating an account, finding a password) makes some of them drop out. The simplest path is a link sent through the messaging app they already use, which opens the training in the browser.

## Translate without rewriting

French labour law provides that documents needed for the job are written in French, and allows translations. So write one French master version, then translate it for crew who do not read French well.

To make the translation hold:

- one sentence, one idea, in the imperative;
- precise values rather than adjectives ("check the core temperature with the probe" rather than "check it is cooked");
- menu product names kept as they are;
- a review of technical terms by a crew member who speaks the language, at least for the hygiene and allergen modules.

## Check at the station after the quiz

A passed quiz proves the crew member understood the rule. It does not prove they apply it during the rush. After a few weeks, plan a station assessment with a grid shared across the network: the manager watches, ticks, comments. The result stays in the crew member's file, and anything marked "to review" points back to the matching module.

## Track and follow up

Head office and the manager need to see, path by path, who has started, finished or dropped out. Without automatic reminders, follow-up depends on the manager, who has other priorities during service. Two sources also show which modules to create: the questions crew ask most often, and [the gaps that keep coming back in audits](/en/resources/grille-audit-hygiene-restaurant).

## Where to start

1. List the tasks a crew member must master in their first week.
2. Write a module of a few minutes per task, from the manual's cards.
3. Set the order of the onboarding path and what triggers it.
4. Prepare the station assessment grid for the two or three key stations.
5. Follow the path of the next ten hires and fix the modules where they get stuck.
